I'm an experimental astrophysicist interested in understanding the universe through the development and application of cutting-edge millimeter-wave instrumentation. My research combines developments in millimeter-wave optics, advanced data analysis techniques, and high-resolution observations of galaxy clusters to investigate the physical processes that govern the evolution of cosmic structure.

I am currently an NSF Astronomy and Astrophysics Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, where I work on the TolTEC camera on the 50-meter Large Millimeter Telescope in Mexico. I lead TolTEC's galaxy cluster science efforts and play a central role in instrument commissioning, characterization, and the development of data analysis pipelines.

My research interests span the entire observational process, from designing optical components and optimizing telescope performance to extracting faint astronomical signals and using them to address fundamental astrophysical questions.
