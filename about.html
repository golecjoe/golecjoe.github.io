About Me

I was born and raised in the great state of Michigan. I began my undergraduate education at the University of Michigan (GO BLUE!) in 2013. I was a member of the C. Wilber Peters chapter of the Society of Physics Students and was president of the club my senior year. I was inducted into the physics honor society, Sigma Pi Sigma, in 2016. I was a teaching assistant for Physics 140 (Introductory Mechanics) when python programing was introduced in a section of the course and a grader for Physics 390 (Intro to Modern Physics) for several semesters. In the last couple of summers of undergrad I was a counselor for the Michigan Math and Science Scholars summer program and was an instructor for a course, alongside Daniel Mayerson and Anthony Charles, where we taught General Relativity to high school students. We published our course notes as a Springer Brief titled "Relativity: A Journey Through Warped Space and Time." I earned my Bachelor's Degree in Honors Physics and Mathematics in 2017 and wrote a senior thesis titled "Development of Laser Machined Metamaterial Optical Elements for Cosmic Microwave Background Observations."

I entered the Physics PhD program at Michigan in the Fall of 2017 working with Jeff McMahon on CMB instrumentation. I transfered to the University of Chicago Physics PhD program in 2020 when Jeff moved his lab there. In grad school I designed and/or fabricated antirelfection coatings for numerous millimeter-wave observatories. I finished my PhD in 2024 with a dissertation that includes design and fabrication procedures for metamaterial anti-reflection coatings for silicon and alumina optics at millimeter wavelengths, maximum likelihood mapmaking techniques for large-aperture single-dish millimeter-wave cameras (including some of the first maps TolTEC made of the Crab Nebula in intensity and polarization), and a forecast for what a 100-hour survey of galaxy clusters would look like with the TolTEC camera. My dissertation is titled "Next-Generation Images of Galaxy Clusters At Millimeter Wavelengths."

I received the NSF Astronomy and Astrophysics Postdoctoral Fellowship in 2024 and took it to the University of Massachusetts Amherst to work on the TolTEC Camera.

In my free time in enjoy watching football, baseball, and hockey, especially if it's the Michigan Wolverines. You can usually find me in the outdoors on the weekends hiking or camping.
